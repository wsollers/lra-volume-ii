\begin{theorem*}[The two-sided integers are isomorphic to the usual integers]
The two-sided integer construction is isomorphic to the usual integer system,
preserving zero, successor, predecessor, addition, multiplication, negation,
and order.
\end{theorem*}

\begin{proof}[Professional Standard Proof]
\LRAProofBodyStart
Define a comparison map by sending zero to \(0\), the \(n\)-th point of the
positive ray to \(n\), and the \(n\)-th point of the negative ray to \(-n\).
The disjoint ray decomposition makes the map injective, and two-sided
induction makes it surjective. The recursive definitions of addition,
negation, multiplication, and order show that the map preserves the named
structure. Therefore the two-sided construction is not a new number system but
a different presentation of the usual integers.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Detailed Learning Proof]
\LRAProofBodyStart
The comparison theorem is the final verification step. A construction may have
the right-looking operations and still fail to be the intended object. The
isomorphism proves that every two-sided integer corresponds to exactly one
usual integer, every usual integer occurs, and the correspondence respects the
arithmetic and order structure.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark*}[Proof structure]
Construct the map by ray position, prove bijectivity from disjointness and
induction, then prove preservation by the recursion equations.
\end{remark*}
